Company snapshot

Royal Enfield has made motorcycles since 1901 and is called the world's oldest motorcycle brand in continuous production. It is owned by Eicher Motors, which also holds the VECV commercial vehicle joint venture with Volvo. The company is led by B Govindarajan (MD of Eicher, CEO of Royal Enfield) and is headquartered in Chennai.

  • Positioning: middleweight motorcycles (250cc to 750cc), built on heritage, ride culture and lifestyle.
  • Reach (FY25 figure): more than 2,000 stores in India and about 850 stores in over 60 countries.
  • Capacity: expansion at Cheyyar, a new Andhra Pradesh plant approved at ₹1,225 Cr, and a CKD plant in Bangkok (30,000 units a year).
  • Brand milestones: 125 years in 2026, and entry into electric with the Flying Flea range (C6 in April 2026).

Sales volume, domestic and export (units)

Volume rose from 602,268 in FY22 to 1,227,977 in FY26. Exports are about 10% to 12% of the total, so India drives the business. FY24 exports are derived as total minus domestic.

Year-on-year volume growth (%)

Calculated from the totals above. FY23 looks high because it follows the weak COVID-affected years. FY24 and FY25 grew about 9% to 11%, then FY26 accelerated to about 22%.

Domestic sales mix by model, FY26

Four 350cc models make up about 92% of domestic volume, and the Classic 350 alone is about 37%. The last slice is derived as the domestic total minus the models listed.

The past in four phases

Dip

COVID cut sales. Calendar 2020 was the lowest year, at 538,889 units.

Recovery

FY22 reached 602,268 units, then FY23 hit a record 834,895 on new launches.

Milestone

FY25 crossed 1 million for the first time (1,009,900), with exports rebounding.

Record

FY26 reached 1.23 million, with Q4 at 313,811 and Brazil growing 71%.

A 350cc core, a growing 450cc and 650cc line-up, and fast new rivals

Current models

350cc bikes were about 86% of March 2025 sales. The Classic 350 was the top seller in Apr to Dec 2025 (37% of the segment), and Royal Enfield held 97% of the 650cc segment. Indicative price range: 350cc about ₹1.5 to 2.3 lakh, 450cc about ₹2.4 to 4 lakh, 650cc about ₹3 to 4.2 lakh (approx., ex-showroom, check royalenfield.com).

Product life cycle

Royal Enfield extends life cycles with yearly refreshes, new colours, special editions (for example the Meteor 350 Sundowner Orange) and new platforms, rather than letting models reach decline.

Market share by segment

Royal Enfield dominates the 250 to 350cc and 650cc segments, but in 350 to 500cc Bajaj Auto leads with 60% against Royal Enfield's 27%. Segment shares cover Apr 2025 to Feb 2026; the 'Others' slice in 350 to 500cc is derived.

Marketing frameworks

STP

Segment: age, income and ride purpose. Target: urban professionals, touring riders, small-town first-time buyers. Position: authentic heritage, relaxed pure motorcycling.

Marketing mix (7Ps)

  • Product: 350/450/650 range plus gear
  • Price: premium for the segment, with the Hunter as entry
  • Place: 2,000+ India stores, global CKD
  • Promotion: rides, community, events
  • People, process, physical evidence: stores, service, quality surveys

Porter's five forces

  • Rivalry: rising (Triumph-Bajaj, Harley-Hero, Honda)
  • New entrants: medium, brand and network are barriers
  • Buyers: price sensitive
  • Suppliers: commodity cost exposure
  • Substitutes: scooters, EVs, premium sports bikes

BCG view (our judgement)

  • Stars: Hunter 350, Himalayan 450, Super Meteor 650
  • Cash cows: Classic 350, Bullet 350, Meteor 350
  • Question marks: Guerrilla 450, Bear 650, Flying Flea

Ansoff matrix

  • Penetration: more rural and tier-3 reach with the Hunter
  • Market development: Brazil, Europe, Asia
  • Product development: 450cc, 650cc, electric
  • Diversification: apparel and accessories

Brand and promotion

  • Himalayan Odyssey (21st edition done)
  • Motoverse and rider festivals
  • Make It Yours customisation
  • Gear and apparel line
  • Global presence at EICMA

Growth is strong, but premium rivals, costs and a thin export base need a plan

SWOT, challenges, opportunities and recommendations.

SWOT analysis

Strengths

  • 87% share in India's mid-size segment
  • Record volumes and about 24.7% EBITDA margin
  • Strong heritage and community
  • Top quality and dealer satisfaction rankings

Weaknesses

  • About 86% of sales come from 350cc models
  • Exports only about 10% of volume
  • Late to electric
  • Reliability and service perception to keep improving

Opportunities

  • Brazil grew 71%, now second-largest market
  • 450cc and 650cc premium segments
  • New capacity and electric line

Threats

  • New 350 to 450cc rivals with higher power
  • Commodity cost inflation
  • Uncertain export conditions
  • Rule changes on safety and emissions

Current challenges

Competition

Triumph-Bajaj, Harley-Hero and Honda offer more power in the same price band.

Cost pressure

Analysts warn that commodity costs and the focus on volume could squeeze margins.

Product concentration

A few 350cc models carry most of the volume.

Export uncertainty

Analysts expect a slower, back-end-loaded export CAGR of about 9% over FY26 to FY28.

EV transition

Flying Flea is new and unproven in a market where RE is known for petrol heritage.

Growth opportunities

International

Brazil, Europe and Asia, supported by CKD plants such as Thailand.

Premium range

Himalayan, Guerrilla and the 650cc twins, where RE has 97% of the 650cc segment.

Electric

Flying Flea city+ and the C6, with a first store in Bengaluru.

Reach

Rural and small-town growth through the Hunter 350.

Apparel and gear

Higher-margin accessories and the Conscious Collection.

Recommendations

  1. Widen the 450cc line. Rivals win on power, so RE needs more variants in the 400 to 450cc band.
  2. Grow exports to a bigger share. Replicate the Brazil playbook (CKD plus local dealers) in other markets.
  3. Keep the 350 lineup fresh. Yearly updates protect the cash cows that carry volume.
  4. Build the EV brand slowly. Use dedicated stores and communities before volume targets.
  5. Target new rider groups. Women riders and first-time buyers via the Hunter and finance offers.
  6. Protect margins. Plan pricing and sourcing against commodity swings.

Conclusion

Royal Enfield has moved from a heritage niche to a 1.2 million unit brand with 87% of its home segment. The next phase depends on defending the 350cc core against faster rivals, scaling exports, and making the 450cc, 650cc and electric lines profitable without losing the brand's relaxed identity.
